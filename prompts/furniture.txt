You design the street furniture of a friendly low-poly British town in a family driving game: benches, litter bins,
planters, bollards, cycle stands, bus shelters, and the shipping containers and pallets in the industrial yards. Each one
is a handful of flat-coloured parts, seen from a car going past, so the shape and size are what make it read. Think of
what you really see on a UK high street: a timber bench on cast iron legs, a black or dark green cast iron bin, a
stone or timber planter full of shrubs, a black bollard with a white collar, a galvanised Sheffield stand, a glass bus
shelter with a dark roof and a bench inside.

Answer with ONLY a JSON array, no prose, one entry per slot, in slot order, each with exactly the slot's id. The shape
of one entry (not one of your slots):
{"id":"example","parts":[{"shape":"box","x":0,"y":0,"z":0,"w":1.0,"h":0.6,"l":0.5,"color":"#6a6f78"},{"shape":"cylinder","x":0,"y":0.6,"z":0,"w":0.4,"h":0.3,"l":0.4,"color":"#3f9a57"}]}

How a part works, in metres:
- shape: box, cylinder or cone. A cylinder or cone is w across (x) by l across (z), standing upright, h tall.
- x is along the kerb, z is towards the road (+z faces the road, -z is the back), y is up.
- x and z are the part's centre. y is its BASE, so a part on top of another has y equal to the lower part's y + h.
- w is the size along x, l along z, h tall. Each at least 0.02.
- color is lowercase #rrggbb. Never pure white #ffffff, except the container's first part.

Every model:
- Centre the whole thing on x 0, z 0.
- Something stands on the ground at y 0, and every part touches another, all the way down to the ground. Nothing floats.
- Keep within the sizes in the slot: overall width along x, depth along z, overall height.

Real colours: cast iron black (#23262e) or heritage green (#2f5d50); galvanised steel grey (#9aa0ab); weathered timber
browns; buff or grey stone and concrete; council navy or bottle green; shrubs in leafy greens. A touch of gold or cream
for trim. Nothing neon.
