You design the everyday cars of ARA Driving's town: the traffic and the cars parked along the kerbs. Each is a simple low-poly model made from a side outline and a cabin, seen at street level from a few metres away. They must each read as a kind of car at a glance: a driver should tell a saloon from an estate, an MPV or a van by the outline alone. The town is British, so think ordinary European family cars, not American trucks or exotics.

Answer with ONLY a JSON array, one entry per slot in the request, in slot order, each with exactly the slot's id. Example of the shape (not one of your slots):
{"id":"example","name":"Example","body":[[-2.2,0.3],[-2.2,0.82],[-2.0,0.92],[-1.3,0.94],[0.95,0.94],[1.75,0.84],[2.12,0.76],[2.2,0.3]],"cabin":{"rear":-1.5,"front":1.0,"rearTop":-0.95,"frontTop":0.2,"base":0.94,"top":1.44},"half":0.9}

How it's built, in metres, with z along the car (tail negative, nose positive) and y up from the road:
- body: the side outline from the tail's sill, up over the boot, along the waist, down the bonnet, to the nose's sill. 6-10 [z,y] points. z never runs backwards. The first and last points sit at the sill, y 0.16-0.36. Centre it: the tail and nose about equally far from z 0. Length 3.6-5.4 m.
- The highest y in body is the waist, 0.75-1.25 m. The bonnet falls gently toward the nose and stays high: 0.3 m back from the nose the body is within 0.24 m of the waist, then a near-upright front face drops to the sill. Never a long wedge.
- cabin: the glasshouse standing on the waist. rear and front are where it meets the body, rearTop and frontTop where the roof begins and ends. base equals the waist height. top is the roof height, 1.35-2.2 m, at least 0.38 above base.
- The roof sits inside the base. The windscreen leans back at least 0.3 m. The cabin is at least 1.8 m long at the base, the roof at least 0.8 m. At least 0.7 m of bonnet stays ahead of the cabin.
- half: half the width, 0.8-1.05 m.
- axles and wheel: optional, the z of the rear and front axles as [rear, front], and the wheel radius.
- van: true only for a panel van, which has no glass behind the front doors. Leave it out otherwise.

Real shapes: a saloon has a separate boot lower than its roof. A hatchback's cabin runs nearly to the tail with a steep back. An estate's roof runs right back over a long load bay. An MPV is tall and rounded with a very short bonnet. A city car is short and upright. An SUV is tall with a high waist. A panel van is tall, boxy, and has a short sloping nose. A supermini is a small, neat hatchback, longer than a city car. A crossover is a raised hatchback with a sloping back. A coupe is low and sleek, with a long bonnet and a short cabin. A pickup has a tall double cab and an open load bed behind it, where the body stays low past the cabin. A black cab is tall and upright with a short rounded bonnet and an upright back.
